## Recommended product location

Build a FayFort-wide **Operations Center** as a staff workspace, separate from the member-facing Client Account workspace. Client Account is currently a business-scoped control plane; the new operation spans FayFort staff, multiple client/member records, and automation. Keep the existing client workspace selector for business-level tools. Gate Operations Center by active FayFort worker/platform-admin identity and explicit capabilities. Platform admins manage policies, queues, departments, and reports; workers see permitted queues and their assignments. Do not grant member or business users cross-member operations access.

## Operating model

Use one shared operational work-item/assignment layer for queues, ownership, priority, due time, department transfers, and escalation. Keep domain records authoritative: a member conversation remains the conversation, and a sourcing request/order remains the request. The operations layer references those records and owns staff workflow metadata; it must not create a second competing status or rewrite client-visible events.

A queue item must always have a department, work type, actionable status, accountable owner (worker or named automation), priority, created/updated timestamps, and a next action or an explicit waiting reason. Assignment/transfer/return/close actions write an immutable history record with actor, reason, and time. Use claim/lease rules for shared queues, prevent two workers from unknowingly doing the same work, and make automated steps visible with last-run/next-run/health state. Waiting on a member, supplier, payment, or carrier remains visible with a due/review time and escalation owner.

Requests remain a linear client-facing event timeline through delivery. Staff can route work forward from sourcing to fulfillment and use a separate dispute case for post-warehouse issues. Fulfillment work cannot route back to sourcing, and member milestones cannot roll back; corrections are handled as new dispute/support work with the existing timeline preserved. Validate legal transitions server-side, require details for sensitive transitions, and notify the member whenever a client-visible milestone changes.

## Phase 0 workflow map and access rules

The request-facing lifecycle stays the member's existing append-only timeline: `submitted → under_review → sourcing → needs_member_input / options_sent → purchase_confirmed → item_in_warehouse → shipped → in_transit / out_for_delivery → delivered`; cancellation is permitted only where the existing transition rules allow it. The Operations Center tracks a separate staff status (`open`, `claimed`, `waiting`, `blocked`, `completed`, `cancelled`) and department, and mirrors domain milestones without replacing them. Moving work departments clears the prior assignment and records a transfer event. Per owner decision (2026-10-12), the lifecycle is forward-only; there is no warehouse-to-sourcing reversal or milestone rollback. A changed product or shipping issue is handled through a separate dispute/support case.

| Identity | Operational visibility | Allowed actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified active FayFort worker | Items assigned to them and queues for explicitly enabled departments | Claim eligible open work; update their claimed item; add staff-only notes and checklist items |
| Platform admin | FayFort-wide queue, roster, and audit | Configure worker departments; create/assign/transfer work; reopen with a reason; administer policies |
| Member or business user | No Operations Center data | Continue using only existing member/business APIs and projections |
| Automation | Only work explicitly assigned to its registered automation key | Record start/success/failure and next action through a service-only API; cannot impersonate a worker |

Terms used by the queues: **open** means actionable and not currently claimed; **claimed** means one worker owns a short renewable lease; **waiting** means the work has a named dependency, reason, next action, and review/due time; **blocked** means staff action is needed before progress can continue; **completed/cancelled** are terminal until an admin explicitly reopens with an audit reason; **overdue** is derived from a due timestamp, never a manually entered status; **disputed** is a linked case, not a replacement request stage. Urgency/SLA duration and alert thresholds remain configurable decisions to settle before live rollout; no arbitrary fixed promise is encoded.
